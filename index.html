Jiahe Cao

Computer Science Ph.D. Student · University of Nebraska–Lincoln

I am a Ph.D. student in Computer Science at the University of Nebraska–Lincoln, advised by Prof. Qiang Liu. Previously, I received my M.S. from the University of California, Riverside.

My research focuses on embodied intelligence, with an emphasis on edge computing and machine learning for robotics. I am particularly interested in how robots can manipulate objects and complete tasks by combining resource-constrained edge computing with server-side computation. I am also interested in multi-robot collaboration and how robots can coordinate their actions to accomplish shared tasks.

selected publications

  1. EdgeServing: Deadline-Aware Multi-DNN Serving at the Edge
    Jiahe Cao, Xiaomeng Li, Qiang Liu, Tao Han, Ning Zhang, and Weisong Shi
    In 2026 35th International Conference on Computer Communications and Networks (ICCCN), Jul 2026
    Abs DOI arXiv Bib PDF Code

    EdgeServing coordinates multiple neural networks on a shared edge GPU under latency deadlines. It combines time-division scheduling with early-exit inference, jointly choosing the model, exit point, and batch size based on their predicted effects on all request queues. Experiments evaluate deadline violations and tail latency across hardware platforms.

  2. CAVE: Crowdsourcing Passing-By Vehicles for Reliable In-Vehicle Edge Computing
    Jiahe Cao, Qiang Liu, Dawei Chen, and Kyungtae Han
    In GLOBECOM 2024 - 2024 IEEE Global Communications Conference, Dec 2024
    Abs DOI arXiv Bib PDF

    CAVE explores how an ego vehicle can use computing resources on passing vehicles. It jointly addresses task assignment and resource allocation while accounting for changing vehicle availability, dependencies between computations, and task reliability. An integrated network and computing simulator evaluates the proposed scheduling approach.

  3. Real-time Adaptive Background Subtraction for Traffic Scenarios at Signalized Intersections Based on Roadside Fish-eye Cameras
    Jiahe Cao, Zhouqiao Zhao, Guoyuan Wu, Matthew J. Barth, Yongkang Liu, Emrah Akin Sisbot, and Kentaro Oguchi
    In 2022 IEEE 25th International Conference on Intelligent Transportation Systems (ITSC), Oct 2022
    Abs DOI Bib

    Vehicles waiting at red lights can become part of the estimated background in conventional motion detection. This work introduces a hierarchical adaptive background-subtraction method for roadside fisheye cameras, correcting accumulated errors associated with temporarily stationary vehicles while supporting real-time traffic monitoring.
